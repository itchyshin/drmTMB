## Judgment — is the un-fired D-43 panel drift?

Yes — and the plan text removes the ambiguity the task's own framing offers as
the charitable read. The plan does not say "consider firing" or "if
consequential"; it says, twice: *"D-43 PANEL: **fires** — this IS a milestone
claim (a release gate). Composition 2 Sonnet + 1 ceiling, fresh contexts, at N6
once the number and receipt exist"* (slice-table footer), and again under Verify:
*"**D-43 panel** at N6 — three fresh reviewers under distinct lenses; >=2
NOT-DONE verdicts withhold the claim."* That classification was written
2026-08-04 08:24, before the number existed — the same
pre-register-before-you-know-the-answer discipline the arc applies to its own
coverage rule. The mitigating side is real and worth stating plainly: the arc's
own prose is unusually self-limiting (`VERDICT.md`: "Does not claim `supported`...
does not revise D-97's accepted pooled figure, promote any capability ledger
cell, or move the census"), so a wrong PASS here would not silently propagate
into a ledger cell the way a tier promotion would, and the publish decision
itself is explicitly left to Shinichi (D-93/CI-17) rather than self-granted. But
D-117 exists precisely to stop a checkmark being taken at face value for a
release decision — D-43's own stated rationale is that "a green test... proves a
feature EXISTS, not that it is validated... enthusiasm launders 'exists' into
'done'" (`DECISIONS.md:925-927`) — and discharging a named, ledger-tracked
release hold is exactly the kind of consequential claim the panel exists to
stress-test. That matters more, not less, here: the PASS itself rests on a soft
inequality (`coverage + 2*MCSE >= floor`) with the worst cell's point estimate
(0.9140) sitting *below* the 0.918 floor, exactly the kind of borderline call a
skeptical fresh reader might not accept as equivalent to "at or above." No
disclosure of the skip appears anywhere: not in `PREREGISTRATION.md` (which
pre-commits to the panel), not in `VERDICT.md`, not in the after-task report's
ten numbered sections, not in the check-log entry, not in the PR body. That
silence, not just the missed panel, is what makes this DRIFT rather than a
defensible on-the-fly re-scope — a re-scope would have said so.

## Recurring drift class

This is the **second consecutive arc, one day apart**, where a planned dispatch
or oversight mechanism did not run as written and the self-report did not name
the gap: the prior reconciliation's R-1 (`d1314dfef` silently folding three
slices, including S7, into one inline commit) and this arc's D-1 (the D-43 panel)

version — dispatch-vs-inline simply being *unverifiable* rather than *verifiably
skipped* — has also now recurred twice (sibling R-2; this arc's D-4). Worth a
line in the ledger: **when an orchestrator folds a planned dispatched or gated
slice inline, the after-task report should enumerate every planned slice ID and
mark it DISPATCHED or INLINE explicitly**, rather than narrating the folded
content without naming what didn't happen. That is a one-line checklist
addition, not a new process, and it would have caught D-1 directly.
