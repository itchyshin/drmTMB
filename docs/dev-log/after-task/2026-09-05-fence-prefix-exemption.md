## 1. Goal

Issue #1224. The A4/G17 fixed-effect-only scope fence exempted bivariate families
by testing their NAME (`startsWith(row$family, "biv_")`), because `biv_gaussian`
legitimately fits predictor-driven `sigma1`/`sigma2`/`rho12` cells. A name prefix
is not a capability, so every later `biv_*` admission inherited the exemption
unearned, and the failure was silent: the bridge fitted a shape `engine = "tmb"`
refuses, converged, and returned plausible numbers. Two leaves hit this
independently within an hour on 2026-09-05 and each wrote its own hand-written
refusal function (#1216, #1217). The job was to make the exemption a declared
property of a FAMILY, and to guard it for every family at once.

## 2. Implemented

* `R/julia-family-registry.R` gains two columns.
  * `predictor_dpars` — **required, no default**. `"*"` means every dpar of the
    family may carry a predictor; anything else enumerates the admitted dpars,
    and every other dpar must be intercept-only. A row that omits the argument
    aborts at registry-build time, which every gate and every test trips on at
    once. A default would have reinstated exactly the silent inheritance the
    issue is about.
  * `fe_fence_exempt` — replaces the prefix test in
    `drm_julia_fe_only_fence_families()` verbatim. Only `biv_gaussian` sets it.
* `drm_julia_refuse_unadmitted_predictor_dpars()` (`R/julia-bridge.R`) enforces
  the declaration for every family, in `drm_julia_setup()`, **before Julia
  starts**. Scoped to predictors only: random-effect bars and `sd()` submodels
  belong to the neighbouring fe-only fence, and `phylo()`/`relmat()`/`animal()`/
  `spatial()` markers are refused upstream with their own pinned messages.
* `drm_julia_family_registry_problems()` is the registry invariant checker. Its
  bivariate rule keys on the FAMILY OBJECT's `n_response`, read through
  `drm_julia_registry_family_object()`, not on the family's name: a family with
  `n_response >= 2` may not declare `"*"` and must enumerate.
* New gate-registry row `unadmitted_predictor_dpar`, with
  `drm_julia_narrowed_predictor_dpar_families()` computing its `family_type`
  from the registry so the gate and the fence cannot drift apart — the same
  device `fe_only_random_effects` already uses.

## 3a. Decisions and Rejected Alternatives

* **Rejected: default `predictor_dpars` to the narrow bivariate set.** It would
  have needed a way to know a family is bivariate before the column exists, and
  the only cheap way is the prefix. Requiring the declaration removes the
  question.
* **Rejected: fence every dpar named in a formula.** The fence is scoped to
  dpars the family actually has, because `mvbind(y1, y2) ~ x` arrives as a
  single entry whose dpar is `"mu"` — not one of `biv_gaussian`'s dpars — and
  because a formula naming a dpar the family does not have at all is a different
  defect with a different owner and its own wording.
* **Deleted nothing.** #1216 and #1217 are both OPEN at the time of writing, so
  `drm_julia_refuse_biv_lognormal_unsupported()` and
  `drm_julia_refuse_biv_student_beyond_native()` are untouched here. See
  "Known Residuals" for what supersedes what.

## 9. What Did Not Go Smoothly

The first version of the invariant checker looked the family object up with
`get(row$family, mode = "function")`, which finds `base::gamma` for the `gamma`
row and returns nonsense. Fixed by looking up in drmTMB's namespace with
`inherits = FALSE`, so a stats-family row resolves to NULL and is treated as
univariate, which it is.

The first version of the test helper passed `.local_envir` to
`local_mocked_bindings()`, which this testthat rejects; scoping the mock to the
helper's own frame is both correct and prevents two mocks stacking inside one
`test_that()`.

## 10. Known Residuals

* **#1216 and #1217 are unmerged, so their bespoke refusals are untouched.**
  When they land, `drm_julia_refuse_biv_lognormal_unsupported()` and the
  predictor half of `drm_julia_refuse_biv_student_beyond_native()` become
  redundant, provided the registry rows those PRs add declare
  `predictor_dpars = c("mu1", "mu2")`. The registry will REFUSE TO BUILD if they
  do not, so the merge cannot silently reopen the hole. Their random-effect-bar
  refusals and #1217's `confint()` guard are separate and remain needed.
  `drm_julia_rhs_is_intercept_only()` is defined identically here and in #1217;
  a merge conflict on that region is expected and resolves by keeping one copy.
* **Structured and cross-family routes bypass this fence.** They dispatch before
  `drm_julia_setup()`. No narrowed family has a structured route today, so there
  is no live gap, but a future one would need the fence moved or repeated.
* **A formula naming a dpar the family does not have** (`zi ~ z` on a Tweedie,
  say) is still unfenced on the Julia route. Different defect, different owner.
* **`"*"` declarations are claims of no-narrowing, not measurements.** Four were
  measured (`student` `nu`, `zero_one_beta` `zoi`, and the `tweedie`/
  `biv_gaussian` cells above); the rest assert the status quo the fence leaves
  untouched, so a wrong `"*"` is an unclosed hole, never a regression.
* `docs/design/parity-scoreboard.md` was NOT regenerated: its generator stamps
  the drmTMB HEAD hash into the output, so it cannot be byte-stable inside a PR,
  and it has no test.
* Regenerating `docs/design/parity-matrix.md` also absorbs a `skew_normal`
  staleness that predates this branch (17 -> 18 admitted routes, 4 -> 5 without
  a TSV row). That is the generator's own reading of the current registry, not
  a claim added here.

## 11. Team Learning

The measured lesson is not "prefixes are bad". It is that the third instance of
this defect was on `tweedie`, which nobody would have found by thinking about
bivariate families — it was found by fitting the neighbours of the thing being
changed. A declared boundary turns "did anyone measure this family?" into "does
this row say what it admits?", which a merge can check and a reviewer can read.

## 12. Cross-Product Coverage

Fifteen registry families x their own dpars, exercised by the generic guard
test: every dpar outside a family's declaration is refused with a predictor AND
admitted intercept-only (both `~ 1` and `~ (1)`), and every dpar inside it is
admitted with a predictor. Families declaring `"*"` are asserted to be refused
on nothing.
