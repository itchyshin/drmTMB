## 9. What did not go smoothly

- Trusting the inherited generator's ARCHITECTURE was right; trusting its DATA (the
  hand-written `st(...)` boundary text for the three ordinary-RE rows) without re-deriving it
  from the current TSV would have shipped a wrong claim (section 3, Finding 2). The lesson
  generalizes: a generator that pulls MOST of its evidence mechanically but has a few
  hand-authored fallback strings for "not yet ledgered" rows needs those fallbacks
  re-validated every time an upstream leaf lands new ledger rows, because nothing forces the
  fallback text to notice the row now exists.
- The auto-mode Bash-first instruction and the R multi-line heredoc classifier interacted
  awkwardly with `sprintf()` calls containing embedded quotes; used the Edit tool for the two
  multi-line `st(...)` rewrites instead of a sed one-liner.

## 11. Team learning

- A generator whose citations are individually correct can still misjoin: cite discipline
  (`pm_grep_line` erroring on a missing anchor) catches a MISSING citation, never a
  citation attached to the WRONG row. The `|` guard is the second kind of defect -- worth
  a full-TSV scan (`grep` for the disambiguating character across every `route == "base"`
  row) rather than trusting the join predicate because each individual match "looks right."
- When resuming a dead attempt across a 45-commit merge, re-run the generator BEFORE reading
  its own inline comments for what it claims to guard against -- the comments describe the
  world as of the abandoned session, not the merged one.
