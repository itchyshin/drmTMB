## Objective, scale and scope

The symbolic target is `V = group_sd^2 * Z*Z' + diag(exp.(2*W*gamma))` and `NLL = (n*log(2*pi) + logdet(V) + r'*inv(V)*r)/2 + prior(beta,gamma)`. Julia's existing Woodbury likelihood implements this marginal target (`J/src/gaussian_ranef.jl:632-656`) and the new wrapper adds `_fixed_prior_value` once (`698`). The normal constructor includes `log(sd)+log(2*pi)/2`; ridge has zero density constant and contributes `lambda*b^2/2` (`J/src/fixed_priors.jl:27-36,66-67`). Priors select only beta/gamma positions; the group log-SD is outside those coordinate blocks.

R's shared TMB objective adds the selected normal negative densities or ridge quadratics exactly once (`R/src/drmTMB.cpp:7206-7218`). The prior is independent of the integrated random intercept, so it adds no random-effect Hessian term and remains an additive factor after Gaussian marginalization. No coefficient-to-sigma Jacobian appears in either prior calculation. Such a Jacobian would change the stated coefficient prior. Residual variances are `exp(2*W*gamma)` and the group variance is the square of its SD.

Data likelihood and penalized objective remain separate: R removes the reported fixed-prior contribution from the stored negative objective (`R/R/drmTMB.R:955-985`), while Julia records `-nll_ml(theta)` in `loglik` and retains the penalized `nll` (`J/src/gaussian_ranef.jl:832-833`). Julia forms the Hessian from that penalized objective (`762-767`). Zero ridge contributes no value/gradient/curvature and stays ML (`J/src/gaussian_core.jl:459-468`; R only transports positive-precision rows, `R/R/fixed-effect-priors.R:126-132`).

Admission remains bounded to the approved route. R explicitly checks ML, family/link, one unlabeled mean intercept, complete variables, unit weights and dense unaggregated designs (`R/R/fixed-priors-workflow.R:24-78`). Julia rejects the out-of-scope methods/options and designs (`J/src/gaussian_core.jl:1033-1034,1066-1071,1107-1110`). No new family, posterior sampler or random-effect SD prior was introduced. The Julia delta reuses the native Julia Gaussian kernel and coefficient-prior helper; I found no imported R/TMB implementation in it.
