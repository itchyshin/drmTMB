## Estimand and decision cells

For each truth value \(a \in \{-0.5, 0, 0.5\}\), the worker-level estimate is the fitted log-SD slope \(\hat a\). The reported derived scale contrast is \(\exp(\hat a)\), whose relative bias is \(\exp(\hat a-a)-1\).

The first look uses 1,000 planned attempts per truth (3,000 total). If it is ambiguous and all integrity and denominator checks pass, a second look may add 3,000 attempts per truth. The second look analyzes the cumulative 4,000 attempts per truth (12,000 total). It does not analyze only the additional subset.

The worker records and verifies the level returned by `confint()`. The first-look replay, adapter qualification and stage-two combiner reject missing or different levels before analysis.

- For each truth, the exact binomial interval for profile-interval coverage uses **all planned attempts**. A valid interval covers when it contains the generating slope; every invalid interval, failed fit without an interval, timeout, launch error, or not-started attempt is a noncoverage.
- At truth zero, the exact binomial interval for false positives also uses all planned attempts. A false positive is a valid profile interval that excludes zero. Invalid intervals are not counted as positive findings; the separate valid-fraction gate limits how many such attempts may occur.
- For each truth, the exact binomial interval for valid-interval fraction uses all planned attempts. Its lower endpoint must be at least 0.95.
- The Monte Carlo t interval for mean log-SD-slope bias (estimate minus truth) uses all finite estimates and reports that count. This is bias conditional on a finite returned estimate, not unconditional estimator bias. The t interval for relative exp(a) bias uses the same finite-estimate set. Each interval uses the two-sided 97.5% t critical value. These t intervals are approximate and do not guarantee nominal coverage for skewed or heavy-tailed errors, especially after the nonlinear `exp()` transform. Missing estimates are not replaced by zero; a non-finite transformed bias makes that metric undefined and therefore ambiguous. The separate valid-fraction gate does not establish that missingness among finite estimates is non-informative.

**Inference qualification (2026-10-19):** for coverage, validity, and planned false-positive gates, the staged accept/fail/continue logic is defensible conditional on their marginal intervals attaining the stated coverage. Requiring these qualifying gates all to pass is an intersection-union test, so their dependence does not require a further multiplicity correction. The two-look 97.5% marginal intervals provide at least 95% simultaneous coverage across the two planned looks by Bonferroni, including nested looks. The coverage, validity, and null false-positive estimands use all planned attempts; false positives therefore mean the planned false-positive probability, with invalid intervals counted as no positive finding and separately constrained by the validity gate. They are not a conditional type-I error rate among valid intervals. This argument does not apply to the approximate bias diagnostics, which have no calibrated interval-coverage guarantee.

**Decision after review:** the t intervals for mean log-SD and relative `exp(a)` bias are descriptive diagnostics, not acceptance gates. They use only finite estimates and have no validated coverage under skew, rare tails, or outcome-dependent missingness. The rare-tail construction shows the risk: if log-SD error is zero with probability 0.999 and `log(102)` with probability 0.001, the population relative bias is 0.101, outside the ±0.10 band, yet 1,000 estimates have about 0.368 probability of containing no tail observation and yielding a degenerate `[0, 0]` t interval. This is a stress construction, not a claim about the fitted estimator. Bias intervals therefore do not enter staged acceptance; accepted results are limited to the predeclared interval-calibration and availability criteria below and do not establish point-estimator recovery.

The acceptance bands are coverage [0.925, 0.975], null planned-denominator false-positive rate [0.025, 0.075], and valid-fraction lower limit at least 0.95. Bias diagnostics retain their ±0.10 reference bands in the output for context only; they have no pass/fail state.

## Decision rule

Before classifying metrics, the analyzer requires the exact look-specific manifest count and one row per planned attempt, with exactly 1,000 or 4,000 rows for each truth. An incomplete campaign returns **incomplete** and no decision. A campaign marked complete may contain only attempt-level `completed`, `error`, or `timeout` states; unresolved states such as `not_started`, `interrupted`, `campaign_timeout`, and `launch_error` contradict that receipt and return **integrity_failure**. A complete campaign with a mismatched denominator, unknown truth/status, invalid field types, or a valid interval paired with a non-completed status also returns **integrity_failure**. Integrity failures stop the decision; they are not model-fit failures. The full summarizer must preserve this distinction when mapping supervisor outcomes to the standardized helper input.

For a two-sided band, a gate **passes** when its entire confidence interval lies inside the band. It **fails** only when the interval lies wholly outside the band. Any overlap with a band boundary is **ambiguous**. The valid-fraction gate passes when its lower endpoint is at least 0.95, fails when its upper endpoint is below 0.95, and is otherwise ambiguous.

At look one, accept only if every interval-calibration and availability gate passes; stop with failure if any such gate fails; request look two only if the complete 3,000-attempt campaign has no failed qualifying gate and at least one ambiguous qualifying gate. Bias diagnostics do not affect this decision. At look two, accept only if every qualifying gate passes; stop with failure if any qualifying gate fails; otherwise report **inconclusive**. An ambiguous look-two result does not permit a third look. An accepted result qualifies these criteria only; it is not a point-estimator recovery claim.

Attempt quality is not collapsed into an automatic health failure. An attempt-level `error` or `timeout`, or a completed but unusable fit or interval, remains in the planned denominator, contributes no coverage, and reduces the valid fraction. A finite estimate may still contribute to a bias metric, with its finite-estimate denominator shown. The adapter must set the estimate to missing when no meaningful estimator was returned. Contradictory metadata is an integrity failure; poor numerical health by itself is handled through interval validity and the valid-fraction gate.

## Analyzer interface and boundary

The pure helper is `tools/general-sd-regression/native-calibration/staged-decision.R`. It accepts standardized columns for truth, estimate, lower and upper limits, validity, and terminal status, together with `manifest_count`, `look`, and `completed_campaign`. It does not authenticate the profile confidence level. The versioned 95% summarizer, first-look replay and stage-two adapter require every recorded worker result to report `profile_level = 0.95` before passing standardized rows to the helper. Monte Carlo interval calculations retain their two-sided 97.5% confidence level. Results without a worker row remain noncoverage attempts in the planned denominator. The helper returns per-metric intervals, qualifying gate states, bias diagnostics, attempt-status counts, and one of `accept`, `fail`, `look2_required`, `inconclusive`, `incomplete`, or `integrity_failure` based on the fields it validates. Bias diagnostic rows appear in `metrics` but have no corresponding row in `gates`.

This helper does not authenticate manifest hashes, seeds, artifact hashes, worker identities, the supervisor receipt, or the profile interval level. The versioned summarizer, first-look replay, and stage-two combiner perform those checks before passing standardized rows to this helper. The historical 97.5% fitted-interval summaries are not input evidence for this decision; rerun results must carry the 95% interval level through the complete replay path.
